# Courtyard Nine

An original Grok composition: a lamp, a stone court, and a circle that never quite
squares. **9/8 aksak, grouped 2 + 2 + 2 + 3**, felt as nine steps with a long
last one. 112–192 BPM depending on the scene. Straight, a little human, no swing.

The scale is **double harmonic** (a major scale with a flat second and a flat
sixth) so the major third and the flat second can share a phrase. The flat
second is a short lean, not a long note on a strong beat: the arranger will
otherwise pull a sustained one onto a chord tone. Land the long last group on
the third or the fifth. A darker scene may drop into phrygian dominant, where
the seventh is flat and the same flat second still sighs.

The frame drum states the meter out loud: dum on each group, tek on the
following step, and two teks inside the long group (`D t D t D t D tt`).
Oud carries the call. An oboe answers from the long beat. A harp zither ticks
the groups. Bass holds the downbeat and the long beat, and may walk into the
next root on the final step.

Three scenes, harmonies bound to their labels:

- **The gate**: double harmonic, Imaj7–bII–iv, a lamp coming on.
- **The well**: slower phrygian dominant, I–bvii–bII, drums almost gone.
- **The dance**: faster double harmonic, the stamp on the long beat, zills in.

A modest ritardando into the last chord. Production: a stone court, the oboe
and the zither wetter than the oud, bass and frame drum nearly dry, a short
echo of one short group on the reed. No drum-machine hats, no four-on-the-floor.

All themes and figures are original; no quotations. This is a checked-in corpus,
so the normal build needs no generation API.
