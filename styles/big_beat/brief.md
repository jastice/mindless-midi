# Big Beat

Written by hand by Carbon, not by `//tools/generate`. For DJ Claudia's pack only.

Rowdier, dirtier and more syncopated than the technos and the house. 112 to
128 bpm, 4/4, sixteenths with a light pocket of swing. Dorian and mixolydian
keys, two on each of six scales around the circle of fifths, so every key has
neighbours of its own and neighbours in all three of Claudia's other styles.

No four-on-the-floor. The groove is a programmed breakbeat: a hard, punchy kick
on the one and the off-beats, and a fat snare on two and four with ghost strokes
filling the sixteenths between them. Crunchy metallic hats, a barking open hat
with a crash on the drop, a sixteenth-note tambourine, Apache-style bongos tuned
to the root and fifth of the key, and a clangorous cowbell. Builds roll on the
clap.

The bass is a mono sawtooth with a sub-octave square, driven hard into
saturation: it riffs across all four beats so the low end keeps rolling under
the snare and swaps cleanly with a techno kick. Above it, a band-passed wah
clav hook that stays on the tonic, fourth, fifth and octave (the coolest
running line, so it is the one the set brings in over another track's bass), a
screaming sawtooth 303 line of tied slides and barking accents, pitch-scooped
brass hits, a rotary garage organ, a gliding rave siren at the peaks, and a
warm formant string pad in the breaks.

Power chords, suspended chords, the flat seven and the major four (`i5`,
`Isus2`, `i7sus4`, `bVII`, `bVII5`, `IV`, `IVadd9`, `v7`) sit inside both
dorian and mixolydian without a third clashing against either mode. Sections of
16 and 32 bars: a breakdown without the low end, a 4- or 8-bar build, a peak,
and phrase-end stutters far more often than in techno. Fifteen parts in all; at
most nine at once, three pitched lines, two percussion parts, two noise layers.

Sounds are tuned by measurement (`src/sound/big_beat.test.ts`). Nobody has
listened.
